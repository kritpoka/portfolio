<Section n="01" title="TL;DR" id="tldr">

- Dorm staff ran billing by hand: data in Excel, then individual emails to tell residents what they owed.
- I designed the UI and workflow, helped design the database, and built front-end modules wired to the back end, including billing for staff and resident dashboards.
- We tested the design with the actual dorm staff and shaped the system around the problems they described.

<Section n="02" title="Context & problem">

Every month, the dormitory staff copied the previous month's sheet into a new one and updated each resident's details by hand — the same repetitive work, month after month.

That spreadsheet was the whole system. Resident and billing data lived in Excel, and staff used it to email each resident their payment notice. The tools were separate from each other, so nobody could track where a bill or request stood — whether it had been sent, seen or paid — without checking several places.

</Section>

<Section n="03" title="My role">

In a team of four, I owned the UI design and the workflow — how a bill moves from staff to resident and back. I designed part of the database and built front-end modules connected to the back end, including the billing features in both the staff and resident dashboards.

</Section>

<Section n="04" title="Users & research">

Two groups use the system: **dormitory staff**, who issue and track bills, and **residents**, who receive and pay them.

We ran usability testing with the real dorm staff on our design, to check it actually solved the problems they had described.

<Section n="05" title="Decisions & trade-offs">

<Decision
  title="One system instead of separate tools"
  why="Resident data, bills, notices and payment status live in one place. A new month starts from current resident data instead of a copied spreadsheet, and every bill has a status both sides can see, replacing the Excel + email loop."
/>
<Decision
  title="Status tracking as a first-class feature"
  why="The main problem wasn't data entry, it was not knowing what state things were in, so the workflow was designed around visible status at each step."
