<Section n="01" title="TL;DR" id="tldr">

- A stall at Kaset Fair needed a simple way to take orders, keep its product list up to date and get paid without cash.
- I built a mobile-first POS: menu and cart, product management, PromptPay QR payments to the shop's real account, and a sales dashboard.
- The stall was cancelled before the fair, so it never ran live — but it was built and tested against real requirements.

</Section>

<Section n="02" title="Context & problem">

The system was built for a real stall at Kaset Fair. The requirements came from the people running it: staff log in with set accounts, orders are taken on a phone, and customers pay by scanning a PromptPay QR code generated for the exact amount and paid into the shop's real bank account.

The stall was cancelled before the fair opened, so the system never went live, but every requirement was real.

</Section>

<Section n="03" title="My role">

A solo project: I designed and built it end to end, from the stall team's requirements to the deployed app.

</Section>

<Section n="04" title="What it does">

- Menu and cart for fast order taking; orders are saved with their items and payment status.
- Product management: add, edit and delete products.
- A PromptPay QR generated for each order, for the exact amount.
- A dashboard that reflects every order: order count, total sales, average sale per bill and a paginated order history.
- Sign-in with fixed staff accounts.

<Section n="05" title="Decisions & trade-offs">

<Decision
  title="Supabase instead of a custom back end"
  why="Auth and a Postgres database without running a server — the right size for a two-week build."
/>
<Decision
  title="Row Level Security on every table"
  why="RLS is enabled on products, orders and order_items, and the policies allow reads and writes only for signed-in users — only signed-in staff can read or change data. It's a single-shop app, so policies are per role rather than per vendor."
/>
<Decision
  title="PromptPay QR in the browser"
  why="The QR is generated client-side for each order's total, so payment needs no payment gateway and no fees."
/>
<Decision
  title="Phone first"
  why="Staff run the stall from their phones, so the whole flow — menu, cart, payment, dashboard — was designed for one hand on a small screen, with a bottom tab bar."
/>

</Section>

<Section n="06" title="Architecture">

<KasetfairPosDiagram />

A Next.js app built from client components keeps the cart in a Zustand store and talks to Supabase Auth and Postgres (`products`, `orders`, `order_items`, all with RLS) through the Supabase JS client. promptpay-qr generates the QR in the browser, and the app is deployed on Vercel.

</Section>

<Section n="07" title="Outcome">

- Built and working end to end in about two weeks, and deployed at [ku-media-pos.vercel.app](https://ku-media-pos.vercel.app).
- Never used at the fair, because the stall was cancelled.
- No real sales data: the dashboard screenshot shows test orders only.

</Section>

<Section n="08" title="What I'd change">

- Add per-role policies, so a cashier and an owner can do different things.
- Turn on Realtime so several phones stay in sync.
- Add an offline mode for weak fair Wi-Fi.
- Add real stock tracking with quantities.

</Section>
